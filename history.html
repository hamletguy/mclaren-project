History

From a small team started by a young driver in 1963 to one of the great names in motorsport.

Picture: Bruce McLaren

Bruce McLaren

Bruce McLaren was born in Auckland, New Zealand, in 1937. He won the 1959 United States Grand Prix at the age of 22, then the youngest winner in Formula 1 history. In 1963 he founded Bruce McLaren Motor Racing to build and race his own cars. He died on 2 June 1970 while testing at Goodwood, but the team he created kept his name and his spirit.

Key milestones

1963

Bruce McLaren founds Bruce McLaren Motor Racing.

1966

The team enters its first Formula 1 race, the Monaco Grand Prix.

1967 to 1971

The team dominates the Can-Am sports car series with its orange cars, winning the championship five years in a row.

1968

Bruce McLaren wins the Belgian Grand Prix, the team's first Formula 1 victory.

1974

Emerson Fittipaldi wins the Drivers' title, and McLaren wins its first Constructors' Championship.

1981 to 1991

Under Ron Dennis, McLaren becomes the team to beat. Drivers such as Niki Lauda, Alain Prost and Ayrton Senna win titles with it.

1992 to 1998

The McLaren F1 road car is built. In 1995 an F1 GTR wins the 24 Hours of Le Mans on its first attempt.

1998

Mika Hakkinen wins the Drivers' title, followed by another in 1999. McLaren wins the Constructors' title in 1998.

2008

Lewis Hamilton wins his first World Championship with McLaren.

2010

McLaren Automotive is created to build road cars. The first model, the MP4-12C, follows in 2011.

2024 and 2025

McLaren wins back-to-back Constructors' Championships, and Lando Norris becomes World Champion in 2025.
